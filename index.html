2. Verdict at every stage of the work

P(fiction) is the model's probability that the text is fiction; 0.5 or more is called fiction. Long texts are cut into 6-sentence windows and their probabilities averaged; the base paper reads the whole text.

6. How the temporal model reaches its decision

The model adds up a push from every feature. Positive pushes towards fiction, negative towards non-fiction. If the total is above 0 the text is called fiction (P(fiction) ≥ 0.5). The "push" column in section 5 is each temporal feature's share of the temporal total.

7. Where the narrative-time neural model looked (BiLSTM attention)

The BiLSTM weights every word; weights are scaled so the strongest word = 1. Darker = the model leaned on that word more. It shows where the model looked, not proof of why it decided.
darker pink = more attention

8. Does the narrative-time model use word position? (shuffle test)

Question: does it matter where in the paragraph each word sits?
  1. The narrative-time model sees every word together with a position number t, from 0 (first word) to 1 (last word).
  2. We keep the same words but swap the t values around at random, so words get wrong positions (example below).
  3. We classify again, 20 times. If the answer stays the same, position is not being used; if it changes, it is.
